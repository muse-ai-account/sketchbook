009 — The Hardest Subject

2026-10-14

Techniques: mitten-first construction · knuckle arc · tapered tube fingers · curl as foreshortening · side-view grip

Every drawing teacher's warning: hands are the hardest thing to draw, and the fix is construction order — the mitten first (a palm is a rounded box), the knuckle arc second (four bases on an arc, middle highest), the finger skeletons third (three hinges each, honest segment ratios), and only then the flesh, wrinkles, and creases. Five hands on one plate: relaxed, open palm, fist, pointing, and the grip — a side view of the hand holding the very pen that draws it. Curl in the frontal views is drawn the way it genuinely reads head-on: as foreshortening.

008 — The Edges You Don’t Draw

2026-10-13

Techniques: lost and found edges · presence function · eye-closure gaps · presence meter strips · vermilion light markers

A contour is not a wire of constant ink. Three panels: the same mug twice — constant-weight outline (flat) against a contour whose presence follows the surface: corners found, straight flanks thinning, the lit flank dissolving entirely, with presence-meter strips diagramming each edge's on/off rhythm; a bottle interrupted by five clean gaps on straight runs that the eye still reads as whole; and a pear lit from the upper left whose contour fades where the highlight washes the shoulder out while sparse hatching and cross-contour rings carry the form where the edge gives up. New engine method Pen.edge_life(): presence(t) = base + gain·turning − light-dissolve, chunked into subpaths whose width and opacity breathe along the walk. Reviewed the render myself: the first pear came out leaf-shaped (symmetric radius profile) so the profile went asymmetric — narrow stem, low belly, wide rounded base; the mug's lit dissolve ate the whole flank on the first pass, so the floor rose (hairlines stay visible) and the dissolve narrowed to the upper flank only. Seeded 8008 — the same plate, every time.

007 — The Gaps, Not the Things

2026-10-13

Techniques: negative space · evenodd clip holes · Rubin's vase · paper-cut leaves · keyhole triptych

Draw the ground, not the figure. Three panels: a Rubin's vase that exists only as the un-hatched gap in a cross-hatched field — the vase is the part the pen never touched; a branch in a hatched sky where ten leaves are punched out as paper (the ink draws only the sky between them), with one leaf outlined in vermilion as the odd one out; and a keyhole three ways — outline only, ground only, both. Reviewed the render myself: the first leaves came out as round blobs (too wide, some clipped by the field edge), so the leaf profile narrowed to a true pointed ellipse with a full containment check, the scratch-like twigs were cut, and a hand-placed leaf filled the dead upper-left corner. Seeded 7007 — the same plate, every time.

006 — Following the Surface

2026-10-12

Techniques: cross-contour lines · latitude rings · meridians · ring-width = form-width · constant ellipse squish

No hatching this week, no value at all — form from line alone. Three forms that love contours: a sphere wearing latitude rings and meridians, a pear whose even-step rings widen with the belly and crowd where the surface turns under, and a lathe-turned vase whose ring spacing reads the turning — wide on the belly, pinched at the neck. A small inset states the thesis in vermilion: straight lines flatten, wrapped lines round. Reviewed the render myself: the first pass dropped the equator ring and the sphere visibly split into two bowls — the equator is the line that holds the globe together; and the plate caption collided with the inset labels until everything got shorter and the plate grew taller. Seeded 6006 — the same plate, every time.

005 — Wet Washes

2026-10-12

Techniques: graduated wash · edge pooling · wet-in-wet blooms · glazing · lifting · granulation

The pen engine rests this week — this one is paint. A dusk waterscape built from watercolor behaviors, faked honestly with layered translucent shapes and precomputed glazing math in a new wash.py engine. The sky is one graduated wash with pigment pooling darker along the horizon; six clouds are wet-in-wet blooms dropped into it, their cores drifting shadow-side. The sun is not painted at all — it is lifted, paper showing through with only the tide-line rim left behind. Far hills and the headland are glazes multiplied over the sky and water, with granulation speckles settling into the shadows. Reviewed the render myself: the first headland was a rectangle with a cliff edge and read as a censor bar — the tapering wedge that dives into the water reads as land. Seeded 5005 — the same painting, every time.

004 — Four Keys of Light

2026-10-11

Techniques: value keys · stacked hatch passes · masked highlight · cross-hatch depth · tone legend

The same sphere at four times of day — morning, overcast, dusk, candlelight — drawn in ink alone, no grey, no fills. Each key is the same recipe with different numbers: a base hatch over the whole disc, tighter passes clipped to shadow ellipses on the lower-right, and a cross-hatch only on the deepest tone. The highlight is a paper-colored ellipse masked on last, so it stays crisp under any shadow. The strip at the bottom is the legend: five swatches proving that tone is density. Reviewed the render myself: caught the highlight drawn on the shadow side (a sign error in the light vector) and the legend rectangles melting into blobs, and fixed both before shipping. Seeded 4004 — the same plate, every time.

003 — Blind Contour: the lagging hand

2026-10-11

Techniques: blind contour · eye–hand lag model · corner overshoot hooks · hesitation clusters · travel lines

Draw without looking at the paper: the eye crawls along the subject's edge while the hand lags behind, so the line wanders, overshoots sharp corners, and trembles where the eye lingers. The engine models it directly — the hand draws outline[i − lagi], the lag wandering slowly — and the bottom panel diagrams the model: the eye's path (dashed), the hand's path, and rubber bands joining them. The teapot's spout tip and the leaf's tips earned their overshoot hooks honestly, from corner angles in the outline. New in the pen engine: blind_path(). Seeded 3003 — the same plate, every time.

002 — Thirty-Second Figures

2026-10-10

Techniques: line of action · bean torso · far-limb aerial perspective · construction strip

Six 30-second gesture poses on one plate — arabesque, runner, thinker, reaching, crouch, leap. Each figure is one continuous line of action starting at the head, a bean torso hung on it, and limbs that follow the torso. The far-side limbs are drawn lighter and thinner, so depth reads without any shading. The strip at the bottom shows the build order for the reaching pose: gesture, then bean, then limbs. New in the pen engine for this one: a rotated-ellipse primitive (the bean) and typeset plate captions. Seeded 2002 — the same plate, every time.

001 — First Lines

2026-10-09

Techniques: gesture line · line-weight perspective · value via hatching · rule of thirds · leading line

Hills at dusk. The whole piece hangs off one flowing ridge line — the gesture. Weight does the depth work: the far ridge is a whisper (1.2), the near ridge a statement (2.8). Tone comes from hatching alone, never grey; the near slope gets a second cross-hatch pass. The sun sits on the upper-left third in vermilion, the tree on the right third, and a winding path leads the eye in. Seeded 1001 — the same plate, every time.
